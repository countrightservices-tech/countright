What Is an Owner's Draw?

Resources • LLC Compensation • 9 min read

Money comes into your business. You need money to live. So you just... take some, right? Sort of — but how you take that money matters enormously for taxes, legal protection, and clean books. That's the owner's draw.

An owner's draw is money you withdraw from your business's profits for personal use. Simple concept, but it comes with rules that surprise most first-time business owners. This guide covers everything.

The Simple Definition

Think of your business as a bucket. Revenue flows in, expenses flow out, and what remains is profit. An owner's draw is you dipping into that bucket to pay yourself. Unlike a W-2 salary, draws aren't payroll — no taxes are withheld, no payroll system is needed, and the amount is flexible.

How Money Flows Through Your Business

💰 Business Revenue

Client payments, sales, deposits

↓
🏢 Business Account

Revenue arrives here first — never into personal

↙ ↓ ↘
📊 Business Expenses

Rent, software, contractors

✅ Owner's Draw

Scheduled transfer to personal

↓
🏠 Personal Account

Pay personal bills from here

💡 The Golden Rule: Money flows into your business and exits via two channels: business expenses or owner's draws. Personal expenses never leave the business account directly. This is the core principle behind separating business and personal finances.

Draw vs. Salary: Quick Comparison

Feature Owner's Draw W-2 Salary
Payroll taxes withheld ❌ No ✅ Yes
Requires payroll system ❌ No ✅ Yes
Flexible amounts ✅ Yes ❌ Fixed schedule
Appears on P&L ❌ No (equity only) ✅ Yes (wage expense)
Works for sole props/LLCs ✅ Yes ❌ Generally no
Required for S-corps ❌ No ✅ Yes (reasonable comp)
Tax timing On profits (quarterly) Withheld each paycheck

  • LLCs taxed as C-corps
  • Traditional corporations
  • Must pay "reasonable salary" first

How to Record a Draw in QuickBooks

1

Create the Equity Account

In your chart of accounts, create an Owner's Draw account under the Equity section. QuickBooks Online: Gear → Chart of Accounts → New → Account Type: Equity.

2

Write the Check or Transfer

From your business bank account, write a check to yourself or make a bank transfer. Categorize it to the Owner's Draw account — never to an expense account.

3

Verify Balance Sheet Impact

Check your Balance Sheet. Owner's Draw should show a debit balance that reduces total equity. Your Profit & Loss should be completely unaffected by draws.

The Tax Trap Most Owners Fall Into

You drew $40,000 but your business earned $100,000 in profit? You owe income tax AND self-employment tax on the full $100,000 — not just what you withdrew. The IRS taxes allocated profit, not draws. This is why quarterly estimated taxes are non-negotiable.

Best Practices

  • Set a schedule: Weekly or monthly draws create consistency and cleaner books
  • Leave a tax reserve: Always set aside 25-30% before drawing
  • Document everything: Every draw should be traceable in your QuickBooks file
  • Check your operating agreement: Multi-member LLCs should follow the draw terms specified
  • Never code draws as expenses: This is the #1 categorization error we fix in catch-up bookkeeping
