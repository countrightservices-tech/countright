Choosing the Best Accounting Software

Resources • Software Guides • 10 min read

Choosing the right accounting software is one of the most critical decisions a US small business owner will make. The platform you select acts as the central nervous system for your company, dictating how you track cash flow, manage payroll, pay vendors, and prepare for IRS tax season.

But with dozens of cloud accounting platforms and ERP systems on the market, the choice can feel overwhelming. Do you go with the industry standard, or take a chance on a newer, budget-friendly alternative? Do you need a simple bookkeeping app, or a full-blown Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system?

In this comprehensive guide, CountRights breaks down the top accounting software platforms available today, their pros and cons, and how to choose the best fit for your business size and industry.

Top Cloud Accounting Software for Small Businesses

For 90% of US small businesses, a cloud-based accounting platform is the perfect fit. These systems are affordable, accessible from anywhere, and integrate seamlessly with US bank feeds and payroll providers.

1. QuickBooks Online (The US Standard)

QuickBooks Online (QBO) dominates the US market for a reason. It offers robust reporting, native payroll integration, and is the platform most American CPAs are trained on. If you want a system that plays nicely with your tax preparer, QBO is the gold standard.

2. Xero

Xero is a massive competitor to QuickBooks, particularly popular among tech startups and e-commerce businesses. It features a beautiful, intuitive interface, unlimited users on every plan, and excellent multi-currency capabilities. If you have international clients, Xero is a fantastic choice.

3. Zoho Books

If you already use Zoho CRM or Zoho Projects, Zoho Books is a no-brainer. It is highly affordable, deeply integrated with the Zoho ecosystem, and offers excellent automation for workflows like recurring invoices and payment reminders.

4. FreshBooks

Built specifically for service-based businesses, freelancers, and agencies. FreshBooks excels at time tracking, sending professional estimates, and collecting online payments. However, it lacks the deep inventory management features of QuickBooks.

5. Wave Accounting

Wave is completely free, making it a popular choice for brand-new businesses with zero budget. However, as soon as you need advanced reporting or payroll, you will quickly outgrow it and need to migrate to a paid platform.

Other Notable Small Business Options: Beyond the major players, there are several great niche platforms. Patriot Software offers excellent, low-cost US payroll and accounting. Kashoo is known for its simplicity. Sage 50 (formerly Peachtree) is still a robust desktop option for traditional businesses.

Mid-Market & ERP Systems: When You Need More Power

If your business is generating $5M+ in revenue, managing complex inventory, or operating multiple legal entities, basic cloud software won't cut it. You need an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system.

1. NetSuite

Oracle NetSuite is the king of cloud ERP. It handles everything from financials to supply chain to HR. It is incredibly powerful but comes with a steep learning curve and high licensing costs.

2. Sage Intacct

A top-tier cloud financial management system, Sage Intacct is highly favored by non-profits and multi-entity SaaS companies. It uses "dimensions" for deep financial tracking that standard accounting software can't match.

3. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

If your company relies heavily on the Microsoft ecosystem, Dynamics 365 is a powerful ERP that connects seamlessly with Outlook, Excel, and Teams.

4. SAP Business One

Designed for small to mid-sized manufacturing and wholesale distribution companies, SAP Business One offers incredible inventory and production management capabilities.

5. Odoo

Odoo is an open-source ERP that is highly customizable. It operates on an "app store" model where you install modules (accounting, CRM, manufacturing) as you need them.

The Over-ERP Trap: Many mid-sized businesses buy NetSuite or SAP, only to realize they are only using 10% of its features. If you are paying $1,000+/month for ERP software but only need basic bookkeeping, CountRights can help you migrate back to QuickBooks Enterprise and save thousands.

Key Features to Look For

When evaluating accounting software, don't just look at the price tag. You need to ensure the platform has the specific features your US business requires:

  • US Bank Feeds: Ensure the software connects seamlessly to your US banks (Chase, Bank of America, etc.) for automated transaction downloading.
  • 1099 & W-2 Support: The software must be able to track contractor payments and export 1099-NEC data easily for IRS tax compliance.
  • Inventory Management: If you sell physical products, look for FIFO costing, barcode scanning, and multi-warehouse tracking.
  • CPA Access: Your CPA should be able to log in for free to review your books without needing a paid user license.
  • Third-Party Integrations: Ensure it connects to your CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce), e-commerce platform (Shopify), or payment processor (Stripe).
